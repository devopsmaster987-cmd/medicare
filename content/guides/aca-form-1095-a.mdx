## What Form 1095-A is

Form 1095-A is a tax statement from the health insurance marketplace. It reports the marketplace coverage a person had during the year and the premium tax credit information connected to that coverage.

The form is not a bill. It is a record. The person uses the numbers on the form to complete Form 8962, which reconciles the premium tax credit.

The form is different from Form 1095-B and Form 1095-C. Those forms report other types of coverage. A person who had marketplace coverage should look for the 1095-A, not the other forms.

A person who had marketplace coverage for only part of the year should still receive a form. The form shows the months of coverage and the amounts for those months.

## Who receives it

A person who enrolled in marketplace coverage should receive a Form 1095-A. The marketplace sends it, usually early in the tax season. The person may receive it by mail or through the marketplace account.

A person who had coverage through a state-based marketplace may receive the form from the state marketplace. The format and the delivery method can differ. The person should check the state marketplace account.

A person who had coverage through an employer or Medicare does not get a 1095-A for that coverage. Those programs use different forms or no form at all.

A person who is claimed as a dependent on someone else's return may have the form sent to the policyholder. The person should coordinate with the policyholder before filing.

## What the form shows

The form shows the monthly enrollment premium, the second lowest cost Silver plan premium, and the advance premium tax credit paid to the insurer. These numbers are used to calculate the final credit.

The monthly amounts matter because coverage can change during the year. A person who had coverage for six months does not use a single annual number. The form breaks the amounts down by month.

The second lowest cost Silver plan premium is a benchmark. It is used to calculate the premium tax credit. The person does not need to understand every detail, but the tax software uses the number.

The form also identifies the policyholder and the covered individuals. A person should check that the names and the coverage months are correct.

## Why the form matters

A person who received advance premium tax credits must reconcile them on the tax return. The advance credit is based on an estimate of income. The final credit is based on actual income. The difference can be a refund or an amount owed.

A person who did not receive advance credits may still be able to claim the premium tax credit. The 1095-A provides the numbers needed to calculate it. The person should not assume the credit is unavailable.

A person who received too much advance credit may owe money. A person who received too little may get a refund. The reconciliation is required, not optional, when advance credits were paid.

A person who ignores the form may receive a letter from the IRS. The letter can delay the refund or create a penalty. Filing the form correctly is the best way to avoid the problem.

## How to reconcile the credit

The person enters the 1095-A information into tax software or gives it to a tax preparer. The software completes Form 8962 and compares the advance credit with the allowed credit.

The allowed credit depends on household income and family size. The income used is generally the household income for the coverage year. The form and the instructions explain the rules.

A person who had a change in income during the year should report the change to the marketplace. The change can adjust the advance credit for the remaining months. The reconciliation at tax time still applies.

A person who is confused by the form should ask for help. The marketplace, a tax preparer, or a free tax assistance program can explain the numbers. The person should not guess.

## Common errors

One error is using the annual total instead of the monthly amounts. The form reports monthly figures, and the reconciliation uses those figures. A single annual number can produce the wrong result.

Another error is confusing the 1095-A with the 1095-B or 1095-C. The forms report different coverage. The tax software may ask for the wrong one if the person is not careful.

A third error is failing to report a change in household size. A new baby, a marriage, or a move can change the credit. The marketplace and the tax return should reflect the change.

A fourth error is ignoring a corrected form. The marketplace may issue a corrected 1095-A. The person should use the corrected version and keep both forms with the records.

## If you did not receive the form

A person who had marketplace coverage but did not receive the form should check the marketplace account. The form may be available online. The person can also call the marketplace.

A person who cannot get the form before the filing deadline may need to file an extension or use the available records. A tax preparer can explain the options. The person should not file with guessed numbers.

A person who had no marketplace coverage does not need a 1095-A. The person may need a different form for other coverage. The tax software can guide the choice.

A person who received a letter from the IRS about the form should respond promptly. The letter explains what is missing. The person should keep a copy of the response.

## Worked example

Suppose a person had marketplace coverage for the full year and received advance premium tax credits. The marketplace sends a 1095-A with monthly amounts. The person enters the information into tax software.

Form 8962 compares the advance credit with the allowed credit. The person's actual income is lower than the estimate, so the allowed credit is higher. The difference is a refund.

A second person had coverage for part of the year and did not receive advance credits. The person uses the 1095-A to claim the credit. The form shows the months of coverage and the benchmark premium.

A third person had a corrected 1095-A. The person uses the corrected form and keeps the original with the tax records. The corrected form changes the reconciliation.

The examples show that the form is a record, not a bill. The numbers on the form drive the tax result.

## What Form 1095-A cannot fix

1. The 1095-A is not the same as a 1095-B or 1095-C. Use the form that matches your coverage.
2. Advance premium tax credits must be reconciled. Ignoring the form can delay a refund or create a balance due.
3. A corrected form replaces an earlier version. Use the corrected form and keep both for your records.

## Reconcile the form before you file

Find your 1095-A in your marketplace account before you file. Enter the monthly numbers, not an annual total. If you had advance credits, complete the reconciliation. The
[ACA cost sharing reduction guide](/guides/aca-cost-sharing-reduction) explains the other form of marketplace help, and the
[ACA open enrollment dates guide](/guides/aca-open-enrollment-dates) explains the enrollment window. If you are comparing Medicare and marketplace coverage, use the
[Medicare vs ACA decision tool](/calculators/medicare-vs-aca).
