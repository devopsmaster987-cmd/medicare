## Two separate systems

VA health care is a benefit for eligible veterans. It is run by the Department of Veterans Affairs. Medicare is a federal health insurance program for people who are 65 or older, some younger people with disabilities, and people with end-stage renal disease or ALS.

The two systems are separate. VA eligibility does not depend on Medicare eligibility, and Medicare eligibility does not depend on veteran status. A person can qualify for one, both, or neither.

VA health care is not insurance in the same way Medicare is. It provides care through VA facilities and, in some cases, through community providers. The rules for what is covered and where care can be received are different from Medicare's rules.

A veteran who uses VA care may still need Medicare for care outside the VA system. That can matter for specialists, second opinions, travel, or emergencies.

## Can you have both?

Yes. A person can be enrolled in VA health care and Medicare at the same time. The two benefits can work together, but they do not coordinate like employer coverage and Medicare.

A veteran can choose to use VA care for some services and Medicare for others. The veteran should tell the providers which coverage is being used. Using one does not automatically cancel the other.

A veteran who is 65 and eligible for premium-free Part A should usually enroll in Part A. Part A can help with hospital care outside the VA. Part B is a separate decision and depends on the person's needs and other coverage.

A veteran who is still working and has qualifying employer coverage may be able to delay Part B. The employer rules apply the same way they do for anyone else. VA care does not change the employer coverage rules.

## VA drug coverage and Part D

VA prescription drug coverage is generally not creditable for Part D. That means a veteran who relies only on VA drug coverage may owe a Part D late enrollment penalty if they later enroll in a Part D plan.

The penalty is generally 1 percent of the national base beneficiary premium for each full month without creditable drug coverage. The penalty can apply for as long as the person has Part D.

A veteran who wants to avoid the penalty may enroll in a Part D plan when first eligible, even if they use VA drugs. The Part D plan can provide coverage outside the VA and can serve as creditable coverage.

A veteran who has other drug coverage should check whether that coverage is creditable. The plan must provide a notice. The VA coverage and the Part D plan can coexist, but the person should compare the costs and the formulary.

A veteran who is not sure whether to enroll in Part D should consider how often they use drugs outside the VA. The decision is personal, and the penalty is only one factor.

## When to enroll in Medicare

A veteran who is not working and does not have qualifying employer coverage should generally enroll in Medicare during the initial enrollment period around age 65. That includes Part A and a decision about Part B.

Part A is premium-free for most people with 40 or more quarters of Medicare-covered work. A veteran who has that record should enroll in Part A even if VA care is the main source of care. Part A can help with hospital costs outside the VA.

Part B has a premium. A veteran who uses only VA care may wonder whether Part B is worth the cost. The answer depends on the person's health, travel, and willingness to use non-VA providers. A veteran who delays Part B and later enrolls may face a penalty.

A veteran who has current employer coverage from an employer with 20 or more employees may be able to delay Part B. The same special enrollment period rules apply. The veteran should confirm the employer size and coverage type.

## Cost and access

VA care may have low or no copays for some veterans, depending on the priority group and the service. Medicare has its own premiums, deductibles, and copays. The two systems do not share a single deductible.

A veteran who uses both systems should keep the paperwork separate. Claims for VA care go to the VA. Claims for Medicare-covered care go to Medicare and the secondary plan, if any.

A veteran who needs care outside the VA may find that Medicare offers more choices. A veteran who prefers VA care may find that the VA offers coordinated services. The best mix depends on the person.

A veteran who is enrolled in Medicare Advantage should check the plan's network and travel rules. A veteran who uses VA care may not need a Medicare Advantage plan, but the decision should be based on the person's needs.

A veteran who uses both systems should keep both cards and tell each provider which coverage is being used. That simple step can prevent billing confusion and duplicate claims.

## Worked example

Suppose a veteran turns 65 and is eligible for premium-free Part A. The veteran uses VA care for most services and wonders whether to enroll in Medicare.

The veteran enrolls in Part A because it costs no premium and can help with hospital care outside the VA. The veteran delays Part B because VA care feels sufficient. Two years later, the veteran needs a specialist outside the VA and enrolls in Part B. A late enrollment penalty may apply.

A second veteran in the same situation enrolls in Part A and Part B at 65. The veteran also enrolls in a Part D plan to avoid a drug penalty. The veteran uses VA care for routine needs and Medicare for care outside the VA.

A third veteran has current employer coverage and delays Part B under the working rules. The veteran keeps the employer letter and enrolls during the special enrollment period when the coverage ends.

The examples show that VA care can be the main source of care without making Medicare irrelevant. The decision should account for care outside the VA and the penalty rules.

## Three honest caveats

1. VA drug coverage is generally not creditable for Part D. Relying on it alone can lead to a penalty.
2. VA care and Medicare are separate. Using one does not enroll you in the other.
3. Part B is a personal cost decision. A veteran who uses only VA care may still want Part B for care outside the VA.

## What to do next

Check your VA eligibility and your Medicare work record separately. If you use VA drugs and want to avoid a Part D penalty, compare a Part D plan with your VA coverage. The
[Medicare initial enrollment period guide](/guides/medicare-initial-enrollment-period) explains the timing, and the
[Part D late enrollment penalty guide](/guides/medicare-part-d-late-enrollment-penalty) explains the drug penalty. If you have employer coverage, the
[Medicare vs ACA decision tool](/calculators/medicare-vs-aca) covers the 20-employee rule.
