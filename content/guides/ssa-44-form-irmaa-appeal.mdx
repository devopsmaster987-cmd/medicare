## What the SSA 44 form does

Social Security sets the Part B and Part D income-related monthly adjustment amount, usually called
IRMAA, from the tax return it receives from the IRS. In most cases the return is two years old. If a
life-changing event made that old income a poor guide to your current income, Form SSA-44 asks SSA
to use a newer figure instead.

The form does not erase the old tax return. It creates a documented reason to use a different
income amount for the premium decision. SSA reviews the event, the date, and the proof. The agency
then decides whether the new income changes the IRMAA tier. That review power is why a form alone
is not a promise of a lower premium.

The form is also not a tax appeal. It does not change your tax return, your taxable income, or a
Roth conversion you already completed. It only affects the income figure SSA uses for Medicare
premiums. If the underlying tax return is wrong, that is a separate problem for a tax professional.

## IRMAA life changing event list

SSA publishes a list of events that can support a request. The list below follows the events named
on the official SSA-44 form. The proof column is a practical starting point, not a guarantee that
SSA will accept a particular document.

| Event | What SSA is looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Death of your spouse | A death certificate or an SSA notice showing the date. |
| Marriage | A marriage certificate and the date the household changed. |
| Divorce or annulment | A divorce decree or annulment order. |
| Work stoppage | An employer statement or pay records showing when work stopped. |
| Work reduction | Pay records or an employer statement showing lower hours or pay. |
| Loss of income-producing property | Insurance, disaster, or legal records showing the loss was not a normal investment loss. |
| Loss of pension income | A pension administrator letter or a plan termination notice. |
| Employer settlement payment | A settlement agreement or an employer statement. |

The event has to explain the income change. A retirement that happened years before the premium
year may not help if the income figure already reflects it. A marriage in the current year may
change the household income even if the old return looks fine.

## SSA 44 approved reasons

There is no separate list of "approved reasons" that guarantees a result. SSA reviews each request
against the events and documentation rules in its instructions. A reason on the list is necessary
in most cases, but the proof has to connect the event to the income figure you want SSA to use.

Two common mistakes cause problems. The first is filing an SSA-44 for a normal investment loss or a
market decline. The form is not a general hardship appeal, and a normal investment loss is usually
not one of the listed events. The second is sending a form without the proof SSA asks for. That can
delay the decision and leave the higher premium in place while the review is pending.

If your event is not on the list, do not guess. Ask Social Security directly and keep a record of
the answer. A local office or the national number can tell you what the current form accepts. SHIP
counselors can also help you prepare questions, but they do not decide the case.

## What proof to gather

Gather the documents that show three things: the event, the date, and the income change. A letter
from an employer can establish a retirement date. A pension statement can show when a payment
stopped. A marriage certificate can show a new household. A death certificate can show the date a
spouse died.

Then gather the newer income estimate. SSA may ask for a tax return, a W-2, pay stubs, a Social
Security statement, or a signed statement of expected income. The exact request depends on the
event and the office. Keep copies of everything you send, and send the form in a way that gives you
a delivery record.

Do not send original documents unless SSA tells you to. A copy is normally enough, and originals
are hard to replace. If you file online or by mail, write down the date and keep the confirmation.

## How to file step by step

1. Open the official SSA-44 form from the Social Security website. Do not use a copy from another
   site, because the form changes.
2. Read the event list and choose the event that matches your situation.
3. Fill in the income SSA should use. Use a realistic figure you can document, not a guess.
4. Attach the proof for the event and the income estimate.
5. File the way SSA instructs: online, by mail, or at a local office. Ask for confirmation.
6. Watch the mail and your Social Security account. A new decision should explain the premium.
7. If the decision is still wrong, ask for a review and keep the dates.

That is the size of the decision. It is also why the proof matters. Without a documented retirement
date and a credible new income figure, SSA may keep the old tier.

## Deadlines and what to ask SSA

File as soon as the event happens or as soon as you get a notice that uses the old income. Do not
wait for a new tax return if the event already changed your income. The longer the higher premium
stays in place, the more you may have to recover later.

Ask SSA these questions when you call or visit:

- Which event on the current form matches my situation?
- What proof do you need for that event?
- Which income figure should I use, and for which months?
- Will the change apply to Part B and Part D together?
- How will I be told about the decision, and what do I do if it is wrong?

## If the request is denied

A denial is not the end of the record. Read the notice and find the reason. The office may say the
event is not on the list, the proof does not match the event, or the income figure is not credible.
Each reason points to a different next step.

If the problem is proof, gather a better document and ask for a review. If the problem is the event,
ask whether a different listed event applies. If the problem is the income figure, ask what evidence
the office will accept for the newer amount. Do not resend the same package without changing
anything.

Keep the denial with the original filing. If you later ask a SHIP counselor or a tax preparer for
help, the paperwork shows what SSA has already decided. A clear record is more useful than a story
reconstructed from memory.

## Three honest caveats

1. Form SSA-44 is a request for review, not an automatic reduction. SSA decides whether the event
   and the proof support a change.
2. The form does not change a tax return or undo a Roth conversion. It only changes the income SSA
   uses for the Medicare premium.
3. Processing times and document requests vary by office. Keep copies and follow up in writing.

## What to do next

If a listed event changed your income, open the official form, gather the proof, and ask SSA which
income figure to use. Then check the result in the IRMAA calculator before you make another large
income move. The calculator shows the tier, the next cliff, and the headroom between them.
