## What ESRD means for Medicare

End stage renal disease, or ESRD, is permanent kidney failure that requires dialysis or a kidney transplant. Medicare has a special path for people with ESRD, and the path does not require the person to be 65.

The ESRD path is different from the disability path. A person with ESRD may qualify even without a long disability determination. The work record and the treatment dates still matter.

A person with ESRD may also qualify for Medicare through age or disability. The paths can overlap. The person should use the path that provides coverage sooner, if the rules allow.

A person who is not sure about eligibility should contact Social Security. The application and the documentation can take time, and the start date can depend on the treatment.

## When coverage can start

The start date depends on whether the person is on dialysis or has a transplant. A person who starts dialysis may have a different start date than a person who receives a transplant. The official rules should be checked for the specific situation.

A person who is planning a transplant should contact Social Security before the procedure. The paperwork can be prepared in advance. A delay in the application can delay the coverage.

A person who is already on dialysis should apply as soon as possible. The start date may be affected by the date of the application and the treatment history. The person should keep the treatment records.

A person who has employer coverage should ask how the group plan coordinates with Medicare. A coordination period may apply, and the order of payment can affect the cost. The plan documents should explain the rules.

## The work record

The ESRD path usually requires a qualifying work record. The person, a spouse, or a parent may be able to provide the record. The rules are similar to the rules for other Medicare paths, but the details matter.

A person who does not have a qualifying record may still qualify through another path. A person who is 65 or older may qualify on age. A person with a disability may qualify through SSDI. The person should ask Social Security which path applies.

A person who is applying through a spouse or parent should have the necessary documents. Birth certificates, marriage records, and Social Security numbers can help. The application may take longer if the records are incomplete.

A person who is self-employed should check whether the work record includes the relevant taxes. The Social Security statement is the starting point. Errors should be corrected before the application is finalized.

## Part A, Part B, and Part D

Medicare has several parts, and each has its own rules. Part A covers inpatient hospital care. Part B covers outpatient care, including many dialysis and transplant-related services. Part D covers prescription drugs.

A person with ESRD should usually enroll in Part A and Part B. The Part B premium applies, although some people with limited income may qualify for help. The premium is separate from the cost of dialysis or the transplant.

A person who needs drug coverage should compare Part D plans. The formulary, the pharmacy network, and the premium all matter. A person who delays Part D without creditable coverage may owe a late enrollment penalty later.

A person with employer drug coverage should check whether the coverage is creditable. The plan must provide a notice. If the coverage is creditable, the person may be able to delay Part D without a penalty.

## Employer coverage and coordination

A person with ESRD who has employer group coverage may have a coordination period. During that period, the group plan may pay first and Medicare may pay second. The length and the rules depend on the situation.

A person who is deciding whether to keep employer coverage should compare the total cost. The employer plan may have a lower deductible or a broader network. Medicare may have different rules for dialysis facilities.

A person who keeps employer coverage should confirm the coordination with both the plan and Medicare. A claim billed to the wrong payer can be delayed. The person should keep the explanation of benefits.

A person who loses employer coverage may have a special enrollment period for Medicare. The person should check the timing and the documents needed. A gap in coverage can be expensive for dialysis or transplant care.

## Worked example

Suppose a person with ESRD starts dialysis in March and applies for Medicare. The person has a qualifying work record and enrolls in Part A and Part B. The start date depends on the treatment and the application. The person confirms the date with Social Security.

The person also has employer coverage. The group plan may pay first for a coordination period. The person checks the plan documents and keeps the explanation of benefits.

A second person receives a kidney transplant and applies before the procedure. The paperwork is ready, and the coverage starts on the date set by the rules. The person compares Part D plans for the transplant drugs.

A third person does not have a qualifying work record but is 65. The person qualifies on age instead of ESRD. The enrollment window is based on the age path.

The examples show that the ESRD path is flexible but detail-heavy. The treatment dates and the work record are the starting points.

## Common mistakes

One mistake is waiting until after dialysis starts to apply. The application and the medical records take time. Starting early can help the coverage begin closer to the treatment date.

Another mistake is assuming the work record does not matter. The ESRD path usually requires a qualifying record through the person, a spouse, or a parent. A short record can change the path.

A third mistake is ignoring the employer coordination period. A group health plan may pay first for a period. The person should confirm the order with the plan and Medicare before a claim is filed.

A fourth mistake is skipping drug coverage. Part D has its own rules and a separate penalty. The person should compare plans before the first prescription. A plan that covers the drug is worth more than a lower premium that does not.

## What ESRD timing depends on

1. The ESRD start date depends on dialysis or transplant facts. Do not assume a single date applies to everyone.
2. Employer coverage can change the order of payment for a period. Check the plan documents.
3. Part D has separate rules and a separate penalty. Do not ignore drug coverage.

## Confirm the start date with Social Security

Contact Social Security before dialysis or a transplant if possible. Confirm the start date and the work record, then check how any employer plan coordinates. The
[Medicare for people with disabilities guide](/guides/medicare-for-people-with-disabilities) explains the SSDI path, and the
[Medicare and ALS guide](/guides/medicare-and-als) covers another exception. If you need help with premiums, use the
[Medicare Savings Program calculator](/calculators/medicare-savings-program-extra-help).
