## What Original Medicare covers and does not cover

Original Medicare is built around medically necessary care. Part A covers inpatient hospital care,
skilled nursing, hospice, and some home health. Part B covers physician services, outpatient care,
durable medical equipment, preventive services, and some medical supplies. Neither part was designed
to pay for routine dental, vision, or hearing services, so those costs fall to the beneficiary
unless another program or plan applies.

The practical consequence is that most older adults pay for these three categories themselves, or
through a Medicare Advantage plan, a standalone dental or vision plan, a retiree benefit, or a
state program. The question is not whether Medicare will help; it is which of the narrow exceptions
applies to a given situation.

## Does Medicare cover dental

In most cases, no. Routine oral health and dental care that keeps teeth and gums healthy is not
covered. That includes cleanings, fillings, dentures, implants, root canals, and extractions done
for dental reasons. Medicare does not cover the cost of dentures even when the extraction that
preceded them was covered.

Medicare can cover dental services in a limited set of cases, when the dental work is directly
linked to the success of a covered medical treatment. The situations Medicare lists include an oral
exam and treatment before a heart valve replacement, an organ or bone marrow transplant, or a kidney
transplant; a procedure such as a tooth extraction to treat a mouth infection before chemotherapy
or other cancer treatment; treatment for a complication during head or neck cancer care; and dental
or oral exams before and during dialysis for end-stage renal disease, along with treatment of an
oral or dental infection in that setting.

Two more cases matter. Medicare covers some dental services when you are admitted as a hospital
inpatient for a dental procedure because of an underlying medical condition or the severity of the
procedure. And Medicare covers dental services that are an integral part of a covered procedure,
such as reconstruction of the jaw after an accident. In each of these cases, Medicare pays for the
dental service under Part A or Part B, but it does not cover follow-up dental care once the medical
condition has been treated, and it does not cover implants or dentures even when the preparatory
work was covered.

The cost sharing on the covered cases follows the normal rules. Part B services are paid at 80
percent of the Medicare-approved amount after the deductible, and an additional copayment applies
when the service is delivered in a hospital outpatient setting. The deductible is{' '}
{formatMoney(deductible)} for {year}.

## Does Medicare cover vision

Routine vision care is not covered. Original Medicare does not pay for yearly eye exams to check
vision, glasses, or contact lenses, and it does not pay for the fitting or purchase of eyewear.

Part B does cover specific vision services tied to a medical condition. An annual eye exam from a
state-authorized doctor is covered to check for diabetic retinopathy when you have diabetes, and an
annual eye exam is covered to check for glaucoma when you are at high risk, such as having a family
history of glaucoma. Medicare also covers certain services related to a chronic eye condition,
including surgery to remove a cataract and replace the lens with an intraocular lens. After that
surgery, Medicare covers one pair of glasses or contact lenses. Medicare also covers an eye exam
related to macular degeneration in certain cases.

The cost sharing follows the Part B rules: the deductible, then 20 percent of the Medicare-approved
amount, plus any hospital outpatient copayment. A person who needs only routine glasses or a
prescription update will pay the full cost.

## Does Medicare cover hearing aids

Medicare does not cover hearing aids, and it does not cover the exams for fitting hearing aids.
Hearing aids and fittings are paid out of pocket. Medicare Part B does cover diagnostic hearing
and balance exams when a doctor orders them to diagnose or treat a medical condition, and it covers
an audiologist visit without a physician referral when the hearing loss or balance problem has
existed for 12 months or more, once every 12 months or more.

The distinction that matters is medical diagnosis versus hearing correction. A test ordered to find
the cause of dizziness or a sudden hearing loss can be covered. A test ordered to fit a hearing
aid is not. The same test can be billed either way, so the reason for the visit should be clear
before it is scheduled.

## Does Medicare cover cataract surgery

Medicare covers surgery to remove a cataract and replace the lens with an intraocular lens, and it
covers one pair of glasses or contact lenses after the surgery. The normal Part B deductible and 20
percent coinsurance apply. The surgery is covered because it restores function rather than being
cosmetic, and the post-surgery eyewear is covered because it is part of the surgical recovery.

## Does Medicare cover dentures

Medicare does not cover dentures, even when the extraction that preceded them was covered. Dentures
are paid out of pocket or through another plan. A person who needs dentures after an extraction that
Medicare did cover still pays the full cost of the dentures themselves.

Four routes are common. A Medicare Advantage plan often includes routine dental, vision, and
hearing benefits, although the scope varies by plan and by county, so the benefit details in the
plan documents matter more than the marketing summary. A standalone dental or vision plan can be
bought separately, usually with its own premium, deductible, and annual maximum. A retiree or union
benefit can fill the gaps, and the coordination rules with Medicare should be checked before
enrolling. And some services are available without insurance: over-the-counter hearing aids for
mild to moderate hearing loss, and low-cost dental and vision programs through community health
centers and charitable foundations.

Medigap does not fill these gaps. A Medicare Supplement policy pays the cost sharing on covered
Medicare services. It does not create coverage where Medicare has none, so a person with Original
Medicare plus Medigap still pays for routine dental, vision, and hearing out of pocket.

## Worked example

Consider a person on Original Medicare who needs a routine dental cleaning, new glasses, and a
hearing aid in {year}. The cleaning is not covered, so the full cost is out of pocket. The eye
exam for a new prescription is not covered, and neither are the glasses. The hearing exam for the
aid and the aid itself are not covered. The person pays all of it.

Now change one fact: the same person develops a cataract. Medicare covers the surgery to remove the
cataract and replace the lens, and it covers one pair of glasses or contacts after the surgery. The
person still pays the {formatMoney(deductible)} deductible and 20 percent of the Medicare-approved
amount for the surgery, but the lens replacement and the post-surgery eyewear are Medicare benefits
rather than out-of-pocket costs.

A third case: the person has diabetes and a family history of glaucoma. Medicare covers an annual
eye exam for diabetic retinopathy and an annual exam for glaucoma risk, both under Part B with the
normal cost sharing. Those two exams are covered even though a routine vision exam for a new
prescription would not be.

## Where the rules leave room for judgment

1. The phrase "medically necessary" is the hinge. A service that is routine when it stands alone
   can be covered when it is part of a covered treatment, and the billing depends on how the visit
   is documented.
2. Medicare Advantage dental, vision, and hearing benefits vary by plan and by county. The plan
   documents are the source of truth, not the summary of benefits.
3. This page describes Medicare coverage. It does not recommend a plan, and it does not estimate
   what a specific person will pay, because that depends on the provider, the setting, and other
   insurance.

## Where to check a specific service before you book

Medicare's coverage is decided per service and per diagnosis, so a service that is excluded in one
context can be covered in another. Before you schedule, confirm the coverage with Medicare.gov's
"What's covered" tool and get the reason for the visit in writing from the provider. If a service
is not covered, ask the provider for the cash price and whether a payment plan is available.

For the surrounding costs, use the [Part B late enrollment penalty calculator](/calculators/part-b-late-enrollment-penalty/)
before delaying coverage, the [Medicare Savings Program and Extra Help calculator](/calculators/medicare-savings-program-extra-help/)
if income is limited, and the [IRMAA cliff checker](/calculators/irmaa-cliff-checker/) if income is
near a surcharge threshold. The [Part D out-of-pocket cap guide](/guides/part-d-out-of-pocket-cap/)
covers drug costs, which are a separate budget line from dental, vision, and hearing.
