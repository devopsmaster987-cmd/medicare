# Backlink plan (white-hat only)

The goal is not "as many links as possible." The goal is links from pages that help a reader
answer a Medicare or ACA question. A small number of relevant links beats a large number of
low-quality ones, and a link scheme can undo months of work.

## What is already built to earn links

- `/link-to-us/`: ready-to-use citation text, a plain HTML link, suggested anchor text, and a list
  of the best pages to cite.
- `/press/`: publisher facts, citable figures with official sources, logo files, and a media
  contact.
- `/data/`: a source-linked JSON file generated from the same data layer as the calculators, plus
  an embeddable data badge.
- `Cite this page` blocks on calculators and guides.
- `/llms.txt`, `/llms-full.txt`, `/feed.xml`, and `/sitemap.xml` for machines and researchers.
- A visible checked date and official source link on every figure.

## Rules

- No paid links, link exchanges, private blog networks, expired-domain redirects, or automated
  comment/form submissions.
- No fake reviews, testimonials, credentials, or "as seen in" claims.
- Do not ask a site to remove `nofollow`, `sponsored`, or `ugc` attributes. A normal link is enough.
- Do not mass-email. Send a specific note to a specific page owner about a specific page.
- If a site says no, record it and do not follow up more than once.
- Keep outreach claims accurate: independent publisher in California, USA; not a US agency, agent,
  attorney, CPA, or licensed adviser.

## Target list

Build the list in a spreadsheet with: site, URL, contact, reason, date contacted, response, link
placed (yes/no), and notes.

### 1. Resource pages that already answer the same question

Look for pages that list Medicare cost tools, enrollment checklists, IRMAA explainers, or ACA
subsidy resources. The best targets are pages that already link to official sources and have a
short, curated list.

Examples of categories, not endorsements:

- State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) resource pages.
- Area Agencies on Aging and senior center resource lists.
- Public library "Medicare help" pages.
- Nonprofit patient advocacy and chronic-condition organizations.
- Community college and university personal finance resource pages.
- Credit union and library financial-literacy pages.

Pitch: "Your page on [topic] links to [official source]. This free calculator answers the next
question, [specific question], and shows the checked date and source. If it fits, here is the
specific page: [URL]."

### 2. Journalists and data citations

The `/data/` file is the strongest asset for a data citation. A reporter can use one number, keep
the source, and link to the method.

- Watch for stories about Medicare premiums, IRMAA, Part D costs, ACA subsidies, and enrollment.
- Respond to reporter request services with a specific, sourced number, not a generic pitch.
- Offer the data file and the methodology page, not a guest post.
- Never ask for a link in exchange for a quote.

Pitch: "For your story on [topic], the [year] [figure] is [value], according to [official source].
The source-linked record is here: [URL]. The method and checked date are on the same page."

### 3. Broken-link and outdated-figure outreach

Find resource pages that link to an official page that has moved, or that quote an old premium.
Offer the current official source and, only if it fits, the calculator that explains the change.

This is useful to the page owner even if they do not link to us.

### 4. Podcasts, newsletters, and local radio

Retirement, personal finance, and consumer-affairs shows need plain-language explainers with real
numbers.

- Pitch a specific segment: "The two-year IRMAA lookback," "Why the family glitch fix matters,"
  or "What happens if you miss the Part B window."
- Send a one-page outline and three sourced facts.
- Do not ask for a link; offer a resource page for the show notes.

### 5. Genuine community participation

Answer questions in communities where self-promotion is allowed and where the answer is useful.
Read the rules first. Disclose the site only when the calculator directly answers the question.
Do not drop links in every thread.

## Monthly routine

1. Add five specific target pages to the tracker. Read each page before writing.
2. Send five personalized notes. No mass mail.
3. Publish or improve one link-worthy asset: a worked example, a data table, or a plain-language
   explanation of a confusing rule.
4. Check the Plausible referrer report and the Search Console links report.
5. Record every response. Remove targets that say no.
6. Re-check the figures and the checked date before any outreach that quotes a number.

## Measurement

- Referrals in Plausible by source.
- Links report in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Brand mentions that do not link yet; a polite note can turn a mention into a link.
- Assisted conversions are not tracked because calculator inputs never leave the browser.

## What not to do

- Do not buy a "guest post" package.
- Do not submit to hundreds of generic directories.
- Do not use exact-match anchor text in every link.
- Do not create fake local pages or fake author profiles.
- Do not ask for a link in exchange for a donation, a review, or a testimonial.
- Do not send the same email to a list of addresses.
